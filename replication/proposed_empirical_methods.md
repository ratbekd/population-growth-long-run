# Proposed CEX methods clarification

We construct consumer-unit observations from the Consumer Expenditure Interview Survey files supplied for 2000–2025, which also contain interviews in early 2026. We identify consumer units using the household identifier and the inferred initial interview quarter, average numeric characteristics across available interviews, and use the first observed nonmissing categorical characteristics. The average unit contributes approximately 2.8 interviews. The archive/member manifest and year-month observation counts accompany the replication materials.

We restrict the reference person's mean age to 25–44 and retain units whose rounded mean adult count is one or two. The main sample additionally requires an implied hourly wage between 3 and 200 dollars, mean reported earners no greater than two, and positive total and noneducation expenditure. These restrictions yield 64,853 consumer units in 139 location cells. The broader labour-supply specification includes 74,902 units before the wage and expenditure restrictions. A sequential sample-flow table is provided in the replication materials.

The education outcome is education spending divided by the implied hourly wage, capped at the sample 99th percentile. Consumption excludes education spending. The wage measure divides reported salary income by annual hours calculated for the reference person and spouse. In the reproduced construction, missing expenditure components and hours inputs are set to zero. These choices should be accompanied by sensitivity analyses that distinguish true zeros from unavailable values and assess numerator–denominator coverage in the wage measure.

The habitat proxy is minus the log of assigned population size in thousands, using category-specific population assignments and a separate rural assignment. Regressions include age, age squared, sex, marital-status categories, race, region and year controls, with log wage added in panel B. Household weights are the means of interview weights. Count and nonnegative expenditure-share outcomes use PPML; logged continuous outcomes use weighted least squares. The children outcome counts children under 18 living in the consumer unit and should not be described as completed fertility.

## Inference text to finalize after the full run

Inference resamples the original state-by-population-category-by-urban/rural cells with replacement. We estimate all outcomes on paired draws to form ratios of habitat coefficients. Numerical convergence is checked explicitly; coefficient magnitude is not used to reject replications. We report the number attempted, number successfully estimated for each outcome, reasons for failure, and the number of nonpositive denominator estimates. Standard errors are the sample standard deviations of bootstrap estimates; ratio confidence intervals use paired bootstrap percentiles. Any unresolved failures must be assessed before reporting these intervals as final.

This paragraph describes the implemented audit, not a claim that the location cells reproduce the official survey sampling design. The manuscript should distinguish descriptive associations from causal effects, and separate a solver/inference repair from measurement and identification robustness.
